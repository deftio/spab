# spab channel capacity

spab 0.5.1. Generated by `node r_and_d/capacity.js`.

Cover text is ordinary English prose (gap density matches real documents). The
secret is an **incompressible** random string — a repetitive one would compress
to almost nothing and make every figure below look far better than it is.

Cell budget 4000ms; `slow` means the cell exceeded it and was not
waited on, not that it is impossible.

## 1. Substitution carriers (length-preserving)

The marked document has exactly the same character count as the original.
Capacity is fixed by the text: a passage has the inter-word gaps it has.
Cells show the redundancy achieved — `3x` means the payload fits three times,
and redundancy is what survives editing.

## Notes

**The substitution ceiling is a property of prose, not of the codec.** English
averages roughly one inter-word gap per 5-6 characters, each carrying 2 bits, so
a kilobyte of text holds on the order of 40 bytes at one copy before framing.
No parameter changes that; only more text does.

**Redundancy is worth more than payload.** A secret that fits once is fragile —
measured recovery is around 29% for length-preserving carriers at one copy
against 57% at five to eight. Reading these tables as "how much fits" rather
than "how much fits several times over" will produce brittle marks.

**Auto-grow trades away the property most callers came for.** Length-preserving
substitution is what makes a marked document byte-identical in size and
invisible to a diff. Auto-grow abandons that. It is the right choice when the
mark only has to survive copy/paste between systems that preserve characters,
and the wrong one when it has to go unnoticed.

**Large secrets are not yet a designed mode.** The sensible strategy for a big
payload is to mix carriers — dense zero-width insertion for bulk with
substitution carriers for the parts that must survive a sanitiser — rather than
pushing one carrier to its limit. That is tracked in `dev/roadmap.md`; today
auto-grow simply raises zero-width density until the payload fits, which is the
crude version of the same idea.
